Growth Areas
What I'm actively working on
📣

Strategic Visibility & Proactive Communication

active focus

The most persistent gap across all four years — and the one I'm most deliberately addressing. The pattern: I deliver substantial work but don't amplify it through active communication. In 2022 it was "speak up in ceremonies"; by 2025 the expectation evolved to metrics-driven storytelling and cross-team influence. The gap isn't about what I deliver — it's about making it visible before being asked. The leadership development plan running Apr–Jun 2026 is directly targeting this with weekly Slack posts, post-deploy reports, and proactive FYI messages to stakeholders.

First noted: 2022 · Present in all 4 cycles
🚀

Execution Speed & Sprint Sequencing

formal since 2025

Named formally by the lead in 2025, but first observed in a peer mid-review in 2024. The specific pattern: spending too much time building automation before starting manual tests, which delays task closure within the sprint. Not a quality issue — it's about sequencing and time-boxing. I'm applying a lean-first approach: run manual tests to unblock the ticket, then layer in automation. The Apr–Jun 2026 plan tracked this with zero rollbacks and 100% MRs with tests.

First noted: Mid-Review 2024 · Formal gap in 2025
🌐

Cross-Team Influence & Strategic Protagonism

active focus

Consistently surfacing since 2023 under different labels: being more opinionated on the backlog, proposing ideas before being asked, connecting quality insights to business impact. This is the dimension that separates Senior from Principal QA. The lead named it directly in 2024: "collaborate effectively with other teams, implement improvements, and lead innovation initiatives." I'm working on this through Inner Sourcing contributions, proactive process proposals, and cross-team QA sync participation.

First noted: 2023 · Escalating through 2025
📋

Test Scope Documentation

identified 2025

A new, specific, and actionable gap raised in Year-End 2025: systematically distinguishing in-scope vs. out-of-scope findings, and clearly separating regressions from pre-existing bugs. This is exactly the kind of rigor expected at Principal QA level. I'm building this into my test plan templates: every session now tags findings with scope, impact type, and whether they relate to the change under test or are pre-existing issues to track separately.

First noted: Year-End 2025

General trajectory

In four years at Questrade, I went through a consistent and recognised arc of technical growth, with a formal rating that rose from Solid (2022–2023) to Exceeding (2024) and stepped back to Expected (2025). The most revealing data point, however, is not in the formal ratings: the volume of positive, detailed peer feedbacks grew from 3 to 9 between 2022 and 2025, while technical recognition peaked in the last cycle. This means I finished 2025 as the most technically respected engineer on the team across my entire trajectory — and still with the lowest formal rating since 2023. The distance between internal perception and formal evaluation is the central phenomenon to understand.
